Privacy Policy
Superhyre Contact Finder

Chrome extension · Effective date: 4 September 2026

This policy explains how the Superhyre Contact Finder browser extension (“the Extension”) handles data. It is an internal business tool for authorized users of superhyre. It requires signing in with a work Google account; personal-email accounts are refused at signup.

What the Extension accesses

The Extension runs only on https://www.linkedin.com/*. It reads nothing until you act.

When you open the sidebar on a profile page (https://www.linkedin.com/in/…) it reads:

  • the canonical profile URL (the /in/<slug> identifier, with tracking parameters stripped);
  • the name in the profile’s heading.

If you click “Scrape full profile + phone” it additionally reads, from that page only: the headline, current title and company, location, profile image URL, and up to ten entries from the experience list.

On a LinkedIn people-search results page it reads the profile URLs of the visible results, in order to mark the people already in your pipeline with a dot.

That is the entire read surface. The Extension does not read your LinkedIn feed, connections, messages, or any other page or site, and it does not run on any other domain.

What the Extension stores on your device

Using Chrome’s local storage (chrome.storage.local), the Extension stores only:

  • your Supabase session (access and refresh token) from signing in with Google;
  • your sidebar preferences (whether it is open, and how wide).

There are no API keys on your device — see “Where the provider keys live” below. The Extension contains no analytics, advertising, tracking, or telemetry code, and stores no browsing or lookup history locally.

What the Extension transmits, and to whom

1. Google (accounts.google.com) — when you sign in. The Extension requests only the openid, email and profile scopes and receives your email address and name. It requests no access to Gmail, Drive, Calendar, Sheets, or any other Google service.

2. superhyre’s Supabase project (npqajviolhoufuggfobg.supabase.co) — the only server the Extension talks to. It receives, as you use it:

  • the profile URL you are viewing, on every sidebar open (a free cache lookup);
  • the scraped profile fields listed above, when you reveal or save;
  • the candidate’s stage and any notes you enter, when you shortlist or queue;
  • a log entry for each phone-reveal attempt: your user id, the profile URL, which provider answered, the outcome, and a timestamp. This log enforces a daily per-user limit and records spend.

3. Contact-data providers — reached by superhyre’s server, never by your browser. When you click a reveal button, superhyre’s server-side function sends the LinkedIn profile URL — and nothing else — to Prospeo and, if it has no match, ContactOut. These are independent third-party data providers; their handling of that request is governed by their own privacy policies, not this one.

The Extension sends data to no other destination. Its manifest grants host access to exactly one domain, so it is technically incapable of contacting a provider, an analytics service, or anything else directly.

Where the provider keys live

The provider API keys are held as secrets in superhyre’s Supabase project and are never present in the Extension. An unpacked extension is readable by anyone who has it, so a key shipped inside it would be a key its users have. The only credential in the Extension bundle is a Supabase publishable key, which is public by design and cannot by itself read data or spend a provider credit — every action requires your signed-in user token, and access is enforced by row-level security.

Shared contact data — please read

Revealed contact details are written to a person graph shared across all organizations using the Superhyre platform, not only your own. This is deliberate: it means a phone number another organization has already paid to reveal is returned to you at no cost, and vice versa.

In practice:

  • Contact details (name, phone numbers, email addresses, and the scraped profile fields) are visible platform-wide.
  • Your organization’s own opinion of a person — whether they are shortlisted, their stage, your notes, who owns them, your call queue — is stored in a schema private to your organization and is not visible to any other organization.

Data subjects, controllers, and responsibilities

The profile data and phone numbers processed relate to third-party individuals (“data subjects”). superhyre is the data controller for everything the Extension writes.

Depending on your and the data subject’s location, applicable law (e.g. GDPR, CCPA/CPRA, India’s DPDP Act) may grant data subjects rights to access, correct, or delete this data. Requests can be directed to team@superhyre.com; a specific person’s record can be deleted on request, including from the shared person graph.

You are responsible for using the Extension in compliance with applicable law, LinkedIn’s terms, the providers’ terms, and your organization’s policies.

Permissions and why they are needed

  • storage — to keep your session and sidebar preferences on your device.
  • identity — to sign you in with Google. This uses Google’s own consent window; the Extension never sees your Google password.
  • host access to npqajviolhoufuggfobg.supabase.co — the single server it talks to.
  • content script on linkedin.com — to show the sidebar and read the fields described above.

The Extension requests no tabs, history, cookies, webRequest, or all-sites host permission.

Data retention

Your session stays on your device until you sign out or uninstall; signing out deletes it. Contact and candidate data is retained in superhyre’s systems until deleted on request or as part of its normal data lifecycle. Reveal log entries are retained for billing, abuse-prevention and audit purposes.

Children

The Extension is a business tool and is not directed to anyone under 16.
